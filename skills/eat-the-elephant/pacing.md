# Pacing a long run of decisions

This file explains why the skill paces and phrases its asks as it does; apply it in how you work, and never recite it to the user.

The skill is tuned for users for whom starting, waiting, holding things in mind and sensing elapsed time cost more than average, as with ADHD traits.
These traits vary by degree across everyone rather than forming a category ([Marcus & Barry 2011, *J Abnorm Psychol*](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0021405); *moderate*), so never describe the user with a diagnosis.
Each section gives general-population evidence first and marks clinical findings; almost none test an agent working with an expert adult, so applying them is extrapolation.
Tiers: **strong**, meta-analysis or several replications; **moderate**, one meta-analysis of lab or correlational data, or a review of small studies; **weak**, a single study or a different setting.

## Defaults

A settled recommendation the user accepts with one keystroke removes both the deliberation and the cost of starting.
*Strong, highly heterogeneous:* defaults shift choices (k = 58, d = 0.68, I² = 98%) ([Jachimowicz et al. 2019, *Behav Public Policy*](https://doi.org/10.1017/bpp.2018.43)).
Keep options few to cut effort, not to avoid "overload": choice overload averages zero ([Scheibehenne et al. 2010, *J Consum Res*](https://doi.org/10.1086/651235)) and appears only under moderators such as complex sets and uncertain preferences ([Chernev et al. 2015, *J Consum Psychol*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcps.2014.08.002)); *moderate*.
No study tests defaults in people with ADHD traits.

## Externalize what must be held in mind

Make every ask self-contained and keep the tally, deferrals and stop point on screen, so nothing must be remembered from earlier.
*Strong, clinical:* adults with ADHD show moderate working-memory deficits ([Alderson et al. 2013, *Neuropsychology*](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0032371)), and memory training does not reduce blinded symptoms ([Cortese et al. 2015, *JAACAP*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaac.2014.12.010)), so lower the demand instead.
Externalizing at the point of performance is a clinical principle from Barkley's model ([Barkley 1997, *Psychol Bull*](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.121.1.65)), supported only indirectly by treatments built on it; *weak* as a tested effect ([Lopez et al. 2018, *Cochrane*](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD010840.pub2): low-quality evidence).

## Immediate results, no unannounced waits

Everyone discounts delayed rewards ([Frederick et al. 2002, *J Econ Lit*](https://doi.org/10.1257/jel.40.2.351)), so let each accepted item show its result at once.
*Strong, clinical:* discounting is steeper with ADHD (k = 21, d = 0.43, adults and children alike) ([Jackson & MacKillop 2016, *Biol Psychiatry CNNI*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bpsc.2016.01.007)); in community adults the link is small and not independent of other conditions ([Levitt et al. 2022, *Psychol Med*](https://doi.org/10.1017/s0033291721005110)).
*Moderate, mostly children:* immediate reinforcement helps more than delayed ([Luman et al. 2005, *Clin Psychol Rev*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cpr.2004.11.001)), and imposed waiting is itself aversive ([Sonuga-Barke 2002, *Behav Brain Res*](https://doi.org/10.1016/s0166-4328(01)00432-6); [Marx et al. 2021, *J Atten Disord*](https://doi.org/10.1177/1087054718772138)).
So never let the user wait on a subagent or CI unannounced: offer an ask that does not depend on it, or say how long it will take.

## State durations

People underestimate how long their own tasks take ([Buehler et al. 1994, *J Pers Soc Psychol*](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.67.3.366); *strong*, widely replicated), and telling people the expected wait improved how they judged it ([Antonides et al. 2002, *J Consum Psychol*](https://doi.org/10.1207/S15327663JCP1203_02); *weak*, one field experiment).
*Strong, clinical:* duration judgments are less accurate and more variable with ADHD (k = 55) ([Marx et al. 2022, *JAACAP*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaac.2021.12.004)).
So state how long a batch will take and how long the session has run.

## Progress monitoring and goal-gradient framing

A running tally keeps progress visible without the user tracking it.
*Strong for self-monitoring:* monitoring progress promotes attainment (k = 138, d = 0.40, larger when recorded) ([Harkin et al. 2016, *Psychol Bull*](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000025)); an agent reporting the tally is an extrapolation.
*Moderate, mostly consumer settings:* effort rises near a goal ([Kivetz et al. 2006, *J Mark Res*](https://doi.org/10.1509/jmkr.43.1.39)); counting preparation as progress speeds completion ([Nunes & Drèze 2006, *J Consum Res*](https://doi.org/10.1086/500480)); framing what is done early and what is left late sustains effort ([Koo & Fishbach 2012, *J Consum Res*](https://doi.org/10.1086/663827)).

## Informational feedback

Report what happened as fact: the user can act on it, and an expert reads praise as noise.
*Strong in the self-determination literature, contested:* tangible, expected rewards undermine intrinsic motivation, while positive verbal feedback enhances it (k = 128) ([Deci et al. 1999, *Psychol Bull*](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.125.6.627)).
*Moderate, children:* praise that is inflated or aimed at the person rather than the work can undermine ([Henderlong & Lepper 2002, *Psychol Bull*](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.128.5.774)).

## Implementation intentions, owned by the user

At a stop point, offer one event-based resume cue the user accepts with y, such as what to type when they next open a terminal, so resuming does not depend on remembering a time.
*Strong for self-formed plans* (k = 94, d = 0.65) ([Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006, *Adv Exp Soc Psychol*](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0065-2601(06)38002-1)); *moderate, children with ADHD, one lab with replication* ([Gawrilow & Gollwitzer 2008, *Cogn Ther Res*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10608-007-9150-1); [Gawrilow et al. 2011, *Cogn Ther Res*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10608-010-9309-z)).
Every study has the person link a cue to their own action.
An agent announcing its own if-then rule ("if a land needs a rebase, I defer it") is a default, not an implementation intention, and inherits none of this evidence.
Stopping at a marked unfinished item fits a related finding: people tend to resume interrupted tasks ([Ghibellini & Meier 2025, *Humanit Soc Sci Commun*](https://doi.org/10.1057/s41599-025-05000-w); *moderate*, meta-analysis).

## What to avoid

-   **Points, streaks and badges.**
    No controlled evidence exists for adults with ADHD traits; in children, token systems do not improve blindly rated core symptoms ([Daley et al. 2014, *JAACAP*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaac.2014.05.013); *strong*, k = 32).
    Gamified learning shows small average gains ([Sailer & Homner 2020, *Educ Psychol Rev*](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w); *moderate*), in settings unlike an expert's work session.
    The tally already carries the useful part.
-   **Ego depletion or decision fatigue as a reason to pause.**
    Preregistered multilab tests found no effect ([Hagger et al. 2016, *Perspect Psychol Sci*](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691616652873), d = 0.04; [Vohs et al. 2021, *Psychol Sci*](https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797621989733)); *strong*.
    The hungry-judges result ([Danziger et al. 2011, *PNAS*](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1018033108)) is confounded by case ordering ([Weinshall-Margel & Shapard 2011, *PNAS*](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1110910108)) and can arise from session scheduling ([Glöckner 2016, *Judgm Decis Mak*](https://doi.org/10.1017/s1930297500004812)); *moderate*.
-   **Leaving unfinished items for the user to remember.**
    Interrupted tasks are not better recalled (recall ratio 0.99; Ghibellini & Meier 2025; *moderate*), so the stop point belongs on screen.
-   **Pop terms as explanations**, such as "time blindness" or "dopamine hit"; they are not research constructs as used, and naming the user's traits is not the point.
