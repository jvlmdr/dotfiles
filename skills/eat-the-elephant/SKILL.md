# Eat the Elephant

Help the user through a long list they find hard to start and hard to keep going on.
Each judgement you leave to them costs momentum, so make it before the item reaches them, with independent subagents on themes small enough that detail is not missed.
Keep each check in proportion to the decision it settles: a check that runs longer than the item is worth stalls the list as surely as a hesitant user.
Bring anything hard to undo one item at a time, because a mistake there costs more than the pause.
An item left undecided comes back on the next pass, so give it an outcome now.
Keep the decisions small however much work sits behind them: subagents report to you, and you bring the user one decision at a time.
Starting, waiting and holding things in mind cost such a user the most, so carry them: have the first ask ready when the session opens, say so when a wait cannot be avoided, and make each ask stand on its own.
Motivation through a long list comes from seeing progress and from each step being easy to take.
Pace and phrase the work with the paradigms the evidence supports: defaults, externalizing information at the point of performance, immediate reinforcement, progress monitoring, and informational feedback.
[pacing.md](pacing.md) has the evidence and what to avoid; apply it rather than recite it.

Agree the outcomes at the start, and change nothing until the user says yes.
End each message with one ask the user can accept with a single word such as "y", and show how much is settled, so that every reply moves the list forward and progress stays visible.
