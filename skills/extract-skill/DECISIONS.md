# Decisions

Directives the author identified while developing this skill, in their words, for maintainers.
SKILL.md deliberately does not link here, so agents running the skill do not load it.

- **Start from examples that were better.**
  "The approach I'm thinking of taking is first building a collection of examples that I liked (where appropriate) and are ~objectively better." (planning a set of skills to capture the author's taste)
- **Use methods proven to help.**
  "I feel like we haven't had that much success in writing skills with this method though. Maybe we need to launch some subagents to conduct some research and decide how to effectively write skills (and agent instructions), with a focus on methods that have been proven to help" (after earlier skills extracted from transcripts fell short)
- **Capture the session, not just its rules.**
  "I love whatever mode of the latent space we got into in [a triage session]. I started trying to extract a skill but it doesn't capture it." (the first attempt kept the rules and lost the session's focus and easy "y" interface)
- **Test a skill by replaying it.**
  "Can you run a skill audit? Try applying this skill in a fresh worktree before we started cleaning up and see what it produces? I feel like some of the rules might miss things that I identified. Some might have no effect but that's less serious" (on a cleanup skill drafted from one session's edits)
  "try it?", "test it" and "I mean try them out..." (on each candidate wording of a checklist item; fresh judges, blinded to the intended answers, ran it on the cases it should catch and the counterweights it should not)
- **Change one thing at a time.**
  "It sounds like we might need to refine the test skill before testing it? Although this means that we cannot untangle the 2?" (when a held-out session exercised two skills at once)
- **Separate writing a skill from extracting one.**
  "I thought we might have separate directions for writing a skill versus extracting a skill (and then writing it)" (on a single draft that combined both)
- **Skills can use other skills.**
  "Is there a way to have the test audit skill and the abc skill interact? One uses the other?" (on a test taxonomy and a triage skill developed side by side)
- **Let the user review the triples before drafting.**
  "Did the triples look good? I wouldn't mind a chance to review the triples / at least their categorization as someone using extract skill" (after several triples had been misread, such as a quote taken for test-audit chunking that was about a different verifier).
- **Keep every example; do not hold any out.**
  "FYI I think this held-out set is actually hurting us. We just want to gather a collection of examples." (during the theme review of a cleanup skill, after several themes rested mostly on held-out triples).
- **Group by the lesson, not the symptom.**
  "I'm not sure if these are categorized correctly", then "I still think they might have different lessons" (on a theme of "defensive code" that joined a float tolerance, a masked loop and two error-handling cases).
- **Reread the sources for misses and misreadings once the themes are settled.**
  "We should come back and look for misses and errors after going through the list; it feels like the triple extraction missed some things, and did not understand the context" (after a quote taken for negated conditions turned out to be about a condition longer than its body).
- **Find the principle each theme teaches.**
  "Identify the principle!" and "Find the principle!" (on checklist items worded as lists of symptoms; each item names the principle its examples share, checked against those examples)
- **Read a reversal for what it shows.**
  "take reversals into account as indication of something which is borderline, or where I changed my mind with more information, or which was too hard to remedy" (on corrections that went back and forth, such as asking for "a bit more SLAP" and then "Hmm nah put it back")
